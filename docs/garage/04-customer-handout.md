# Your car's DPF check: what we did and what it means

*(Garage name, date, registration, odometer: fill in.)*

**What we fitted.** A small read-only recorder plugged into your car's diagnostic socket. It listens to numbers the engine computer already reports and saves them. It does not change anything in your car and cannot clear warnings.

**What is a DPF?** The diesel particulate filter catches soot from the exhaust. It cleans itself by burning the soot off (a "regeneration") when the exhaust gets hot enough for long enough. Lots of short journeys stop this from happening, so soot builds up.

**What we recorded.**
- The engine computer's estimate of soot in the filter
- Pressure across the filter
- Exhaust temperature
- Distance since the last self-clean
- Speed, engine speed, temperatures

**What the results mean.**
- Soot going up over several short trips is normal for short-trip driving. It does not by itself mean the filter is faulty.
- A self-clean that starts and stops partway is common on short trips.
- We compare before and after any work at the same conditions. Numbers are supporting evidence next to our normal checks, not a final verdict.

**What helps.**
- A longer drive at steady higher speed now and then, if your handbook recommends it and it is safe and legal to do so.
- If a self-clean is happening (higher idle, fan running, smell, warning light per your handbook), try not to switch the engine off until it finishes.
- Do not ignore warning lights. Follow your handbook and contact us.

**Please note.**
- The recorder's figures are estimates from your car's computer and have only been checked against a small number of cars. It is not a safety device and we give no guarantee from it alone.
- Do not let it distract you while driving.
